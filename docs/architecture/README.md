# Architecture

The backend's architecture style, its deployables and its module cut, agreed with the user in the brainstorm of 2026-10-29, amended on 2026-10-30 after an end-to-end walkthrough of an unattended price watch, on 2026-10-31 after one of a grocery order at a merchant with its own MCP server, and on 2026-11-19 when the product became a standalone EU consumer app paying through provider tokens on the user's stored card. The decisions are recorded in [ADR-0006](../adr/ADR-0006-architecture-style-and-module-cut.md) (style, deployables, module cut), [ADR-0007](../adr/ADR-0007-cart-evidence-and-auto-purchase.md) (where the cart comes from and when a purchase may go ahead without asking), [ADR-0008](../adr/ADR-0008-merchant-connectors-and-the-connector-catalogue.md) (merchant connectors, the connector catalogue and the connector gateway) and [ADR-0010](../adr/ADR-0010-standalone-app-and-provider-payment-tokens.md) (the standalone app, the `PaymentProvider` port and the payment token, which supersede the bank, the networks, the hook responder and the pre-approval of ADR-0006). These pages explain them; where they differ, the ADRs win.

## In one paragraph
A **service-based** system of four backend deployables plus the web app: an untrusted **agent harness** (chat, the user's chosen LLM, shopping tools, memory, the merchant scan); a thin **control MCP server** that is the only door from the untrusted side into the control layer; a thin **connector gateway** through which the agent may read the catalogues of admitted merchants and nothing else; and a trusted **control backend** built as a **hexagonal modular monolith** (mandates, decisions, ledger, payments and checkout, audit, reconciliation), which also receives the payment provider's signed webhooks. After an APPROVE the backend issues one **payment token** through the provider (Stripe Shared Payment Tokens first) on the card or wallet the user saved, scoped to that shop, at most the approved amount, in the cart's currency, with a short expiry; the shop charges it, the user's bank pays, and no money passes through us. One PostgreSQL instance serves the control backend, with a schema per module and a role per deployable; the harness has its own database. Inside the backend, modules talk through in-process calls and through events made reliable by a **transactional outbox**; no message broker in v1. The decision core is a pure Java module with no dependencies, so invariant 9 is enforced by the compiler. One multi-tenant installation runs in an EU cloud.
