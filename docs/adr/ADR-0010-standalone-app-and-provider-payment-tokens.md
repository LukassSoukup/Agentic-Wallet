# ADR-0010: Standalone app, payment through provider tokens on the user's stored card

## Context
Until 2026-11-19 the product was delivered to banks first: the cardholder used it inside a bank's app, we ran under the bank's network programme membership, paid through Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay, and the bank's processor called our hook responder as a second line of defence ([ADR-0006](ADR-0006-architecture-style-and-module-cut.md)). On 2026-11-19 the user reoriented the product: **a standalone consumer application** (our agent and our decision engine), paying through a **third-party payment provider** (Stripe, Google Pay or similar) with **the card or wallet the user already has**. Banks become a later channel. Users are **EU-wide**.

The user's constraints, verbatim in substance: we never take on licensing and never handle payment details; we never hold customer money; we never make the user open and fund an account at a provider they never heard of.

Research of the same day (Stripe, EU card issuers, EU payment law, agent checkout reach; the report and its sources are in [docs/research/payments-for-a-standalone-app.md](../research/payments-for-a-standalone-app.md)) found:
- a **one-time virtual card per purchase** does not fit: an issuer under our programme needs a balance funded by us (licensing, custody) or a new account per user (rejected by the user); and the card number must still reach the shop, by form filling (forbidden) or through our servers (card data). No bank, Revolut, Česká spořitelna or Komerční banka among them, lets an outside app create a one-time card for its customer;
- **Stripe Shared Payment Tokens (SPT)** fit every constraint: the user saves a card, Google Pay, Apple Pay, Link or Klarna through Stripe's own Payment Element, we hold only Stripe's reference; after a decision we issue a token to one seller's Stripe profile with a maximum amount, a currency and an expiry, revocable; the seller charges it through Stripe; Stripe sends us `requires_action`, `active`, `used` and `deactivated` events. Preview in 32 European countries plus Canada and the US under the "Agentic Commerce Agent Services Preview" terms; Stripe may use Visa's and Mastercard's agent tokens underneath. Only sellers that integrated SPT acceptance (through UCP, ACP or directly) can take it; no public count of EU sellers exists;
- under PSD2 the design stays outside payment licensing on the **technical service provider** exclusion only while we never possess or control funds; a lawyer must confirm before live payments. PSD3 and the PSR are agreed, not yet applicable;
- Google's **Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP)** with AP2 mandates returns a merchant-signed checkout, which is stronger cart evidence than an unsigned session.

An end-to-end walkthrough (shoes up to a limit, a foreign-currency shop, 3-D Secure at redemption, double capture, revocation after APPROVE, duplicate delivery) was run by the architect and independently by the reviewer agent and reported to the user before this ADR; the user decided the open questions on 2026-11-19.

## Options considered
1. **Provider tokens on the user's stored payment method, Stripe SPT as the first adapter (chosen).** No licence, no funds, no card data; reach limited to sellers that accept the tokens.
2. One-time virtual card from a card issuer (Stripe Issuing, Swan, Weavr): consumer issuing in the EEA needs a funded balance or a new account per user, and the number must reach the shop. Rejected by the user.
3. Direct network agent tokens (Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay): in Europe only as bank-led pilots; no self-serve path for a non-bank platform. Kept as a later adapter through a partner.
4. Google Pay as the credential provider through UCP: today only for Google's own agent and in the US. Kept as a later adapter when Google opens the role.
5. Keep the bank-first design: contradicts the user's direction.

## Decision
- **Channel (user, 2026-11-19).** A standalone consumer app with local accounts as production identity. Banks are a later channel: nothing bank-specific is built now, and the design keeps the door open because a decision already produces token terms that a future bank-side check could consume, and payment sits behind a port.
- **Payment through a provider token (user, 2026-11-19).** `payments` gains a `PaymentProvider` port with one adapter now, Stripe SPT, and a simulator for tests: save a payment method (the provider's own form in the web app; we store its reference, never card data), issue a scoped token, revoke it, read its state. Network agent tokens through a partner and Google Pay are later adapters of the same port. The **route** is which of the user's saved payment methods pays, by fixed rules over the mandate's allowed methods and the user's preference order; the decision records and re-validates it as before.
- **Token terms are a pure function of the decision:** the seller's provider identity from the merchant directory, the maximum amount equal to the approved cart total in the cart's currency (exact, in the provider's minor units, converted in the adapter), that currency, and an expiry at the earlier of the stored checkout session expiry and the decision time plus the attempt window from `settings`. They are stored in the `DecisionInput` and replay. The token replaces the **pre-approval**: there is no hook responder, no `authorisation` module and no pre-approval in v1.
- **The second line of defence** is the provider's enforcement of the token's limits (one seller, at most the approved amount, short expiry, revocable). Its limits are set by our backend, so the app describes it as what it is ("this shop can charge at most €90, until 10:30"), never as an independent second line.
- **Checkout.** The backend opens the checkout session at the shop's own UCP or ACP endpoint, verifies and stores a merchant-signed checkout where the protocol provides one (a signed checkout that fails verification is no tier-1 evidence, so DECLINE "can't buy here"), decides on that cart (ADR-0007 tiers), and after APPROVE runs the checkout attempt: `CREATED → TOKEN_ISSUED → COMPLETING → COMPLETED`, with `PENDING_USER`, `UNKNOWN`, `FAILED` and `CANCELLED` as in [06-consistency.md](../architecture/06-consistency.md). At most one token per decision (the provider's idempotency key derives from the attempt id). After the first confirmed use the token is deactivated.
- **3-D Secure at redemption** puts the attempt in `PENDING_USER`; the user completes the bank's challenge in our web app through the provider's own SDK. When the user is away (a standing mandate) and the challenge is not completed before the cart or token expires, the purchase lapses, the budget is released and the user is told (user, 2026-11-19).
- **Release only when the token is dead.** A reservation is released on `FAILED` or `CANCELLED` only once the token is revoked, deactivated or expired, so budget never frees while money can still move.
- **Revocation and stop** also revoke an unused token: the attempt becomes `CANCELLED` once the revocation is confirmed; if the token was already used it becomes `COMPLETED`. This replaces "attempts past the credential run to their end" of ADR-0006 for provider tokens.
- **No funds, no charge rights.** The provider key the backend holds is restricted to saving payment methods and issuing, revoking and reading tokens; it has no right to create charges, refunds or payouts, so the backend cannot pull money into our balance. A build test asserts that no charge call exists in the adapter. No running process holds an unrestricted key, and the provider dashboard roles that could charge a saved method are limited to named people and audited. Two questions go to the legal opinion (T-043): the account owner's residual ability to charge saved methods, and whether issuing tokens under a standing mandate, without a step by the user for each purchase, counts as control over funds.
- **Inbound provider events.** The backend exposes one public, signature-verified path for the provider's webhooks; events are deduplicated by event id and recorded insert-only. Shop order webhooks (UCP, ACP) are untrusted, merchant-reported data.
- **Reconciliation** matches the decision, the token's events and the shop's order record. We cannot see the amount billed in the card's currency, chargebacks, charges outside our token, or a refund the shop does not report; mismatches we can see open a case with drafted dispute evidence the user files with their bank. A refund the shop reports in its order record shows in activity, labelled merchant-reported; it does not put money back into the budget automatically in v1. This supersedes ADR-0005.
- **Merchant directory and trust.** No network registry exists to take merchant addresses from. The agent supplies a shop's domain; the backend fetches only the protocol's fixed discovery document at that domain's HTTPS origin (UCP `/.well-known/ucp` or the ACP equivalent), under the existing fence (public addresses only, no redirects off the origin, size and schema limits), and takes the checkout endpoints and the seller's provider identity from there only, never from a session response, a page or the agent. The backend keeps a record of shops users bought from successfully, keyed by the shop's domain together with the seller's provider identity from its discovery document; a change of either makes it a new shop. **The first purchase by a user at a shop where they never bought through us is at least STEP_UP** (a fixed rule, more cautious only), handled like any open requirement under the mandate's "when unsure" policy: on a standing mandate set to "decline and tell me" it declines and tells the user.
- **Merchant scan (user, 2026-11-19).** When a search returns a shop the user never bought from through us, the harness also looks up the shop's reviews and rating, ranks results with it and tells the user. Reviews are untrusted text: the rating may order results and raise caution, never permit.
- **Currency (user, 2026-11-19).** Each user has a base currency, set at sign-up from their location, EUR by default, changeable by the user (a change applies from the next budget period). Mandates and search are never restricted to one currency. Invariant 6 stands: before every comparison, amounts are converted into the currency of the limit they are compared with (the mandate's confirmed currency; the base currency for the global budget and for new mandates), half-even to 2 dp, with the foreign-currency margin kept inside the hard rule only (ADR-0006, its `converted` now meaning the amount in the limit's currency; an amount already in the currency of the limit it is compared with has margin zero). Mandate amounts keep the currency the user confirmed them in; budget periods count in that currency, each purchase converted into it at the decision's rate, so changing the base currency never changes a confirmed mandate (the user drafts a new version through the confirm screen if they want one). The user always sees the shop's own price and the currency their card will be charged in; a converted figure appears only labelled as our rules' check, never as the price.
- **Personal data before the decision.** Opening a session to price shipping and VAT sends the shop only country and postcode where its protocol allows; the full address goes after APPROVE from the recorded profile version. Where a shop needs the full address to price, it is sent and the disclosure is recorded.
- **Deployment.** One multi-tenant installation in an EU cloud; every lookup is scoped to the caller's cardholder. We are the GDPR data controller; retention and erasure need their own ADR before the first live user.
- **Merchant connectors (ADR-0008)** stay after v1. The bank-issued agent card and the hook move to "banks later"; a connector order paid with the card on file at the merchant has one line of defence and is labelled so, unless the connector merchant accepts provider tokens.
- **Legal opinion before live payments** on the technical service provider exclusion, our control over funds, the SPT preview terms and our GDPR duties.

**Supersedes** [ADR-0004](ADR-0004-payment-methods-authorisation-rails.md) and [ADR-0005](ADR-0005-reconciliation-from-capture-files.md). **Supersedes in [ADR-0006](ADR-0006-architecture-style-and-module-cut.md)** every part that assumes a bank, a card network, a network credential or clearing data, namely: the `hook-responder` deployable, the `authorisation` module and the pre-approval, the shared-instance rule between backend and hook responder, the hook scope, merchant addresses from the networks' registries, the card-and-network route, revocation of attempts past the credential, one instance per bank, the processor in the cross-process traffic, the single-credential request and card enrolment with token references in `agents`, the bank's connector enablement in `settings` (now the operator's), cancelling a network instruction on fulfilment, the bank's app as a notification channel, flagging a cleared billing amount above the limit (we cannot see it), deferring retention and erasure without a deadline (now due before the first live user), and the base currency as the target of `converted` in the foreign-currency margin with its margin-zero case (now the currency of the limit compared with). The rest of ADR-0006 stands. **Amends** [ADR-0007](ADR-0007-cart-evidence-and-auto-purchase.md) (sessions straight at the shop's UCP or ACP endpoint; the merchant-signed checkout), [ADR-0008](ADR-0008-merchant-connectors-and-the-connector-catalogue.md) (as above) and [ADR-0009](ADR-0009-tech-stack-revision.md) (EUR default, the provider's restricted key and webhook secret, no hook responder).

## Consequences
- \+ No licence, no customer funds, no card data on our side; the money goes from the user's bank to the shop.
- \+ The user keeps their own card and wallet; nothing new to open or fund.
- \+ A revocation can now stop an approved purchase until the shop charges.
- \+ One deployable fewer (no hook responder), no 300 ms budget, no shared-instance constraint.
- − Reach: only shops that run UCP or ACP checkout and accept the provider's tokens; few in the EU today. The PoC runs on our demo shop in Stripe test mode.
- − The second line is configured by us; the bank's independent check is gone until banks return.
- − Blind spots: the billed amount in the card's currency, refunds, chargebacks, charges outside our token.
- − 3-D Secure at redemption can make unattended purchases lapse in the EU.
- − SPT is a preview product: its terms, limits and country list can change; whether its maximum caps one charge or the total is verified in T-005.
- − We are the data controller; erasure is no longer deferrable past the first live user.
- − ADR-0010 was reserved for the T-003 domain model; that ADR becomes ADR-0011.
